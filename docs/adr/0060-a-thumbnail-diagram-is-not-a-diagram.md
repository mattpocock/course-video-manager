# A Thumbnail Diagram is not a Diagram

A **Thumbnail** gets its drawing edited in place, beside the author's face, so it needs a tldraw scene of its own. We considered making that scene part of the Diagram model: the Thumbnail pins a **DiagramSnapshot** the way a **Clip** does (ADR 0003's Video : Clip pattern), forked from a Diagram's head on save. That would have given thumbnails search, the Snapshot Timeline and reuse for free. Decision: keep the streams separate. A Thumbnail owns a **Thumbnail Diagram**, a tldraw `document` (same format as `headScene` and snapshot scenes) that is edited in place, has no snapshots, does not appear in Playground Home, and is never subject to the Snapshot Rule. It shares everything at the tldraw level: `CVM_SHAPE_UTILS`, Components, Icons, rendering.

We chose this because a thumbnail has no Clips, so the snapshot machinery would only add rules to think about without telling us anything, and because Diagrams are vector-only (ADR 0003, "Asset policy for v1") while a Thumbnail Diagram may need image shapes for the face. We also had no concrete use case for keeping thumbnails inside the Diagram model.

The model must not lose information. Whenever a Thumbnail Diagram starts as a copy of a Diagram, a DiagramSnapshot or another Thumbnail Diagram, it records which one. That keeps a later merge cheap: both sides store the same JSON, so turning Thumbnail Diagrams into Diagram rows is a migration, not a rewrite. Separating the two after mixing them would be the hard direction.
